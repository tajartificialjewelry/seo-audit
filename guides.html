How to write a title tag that earns clicks

The title tag is the blue headline people see in search results. It is one of the few things you fully control.

  • Keep it near 50 to 60 characters so it is not cut off.
  • Put the main keyword near the start.
  • Make every title unique. Two pages with the same title compete with each other.
  • Give a reason to click: a benefit, a number, or something specific.
  • Write for people. Repeating keywords hurts more than it helps.

Example: Custom Cakes in Lahore: Fresh Handmade Designs | Mybakery. Google sometimes rewrites titles, so treat yours as the best suggestion to it.

Why is my website not ranking? A 7-step checklist

  1. Check it is indexed. Search site:yourdomain.com. If nothing appears, Google has not indexed it.
  2. Look for blockers. A noindex tag or a robots.txt rule with Disallow: / hides your site.
  3. Give each page one clear keyword.
  4. Fix titles, descriptions and headings so they match that keyword.
  5. Add real content. Pages under about 300 words rarely answer a question fully.
  6. Link your pages to each other with descriptive anchor text.
  7. Be patient and track. New sites often need weeks or months. Check positions regularly.

Keyword research for beginners

Keyword research means finding the words people type, then matching a page to each one.

  • Start with your customers’ words, not industry jargon.
  • Use autocomplete. The suggestions that appear while you type are real searches.
  • Collect questions. They make good headings and FAQ sections.
  • Check intent. Is the person trying to buy, learn, or compare? Build the page to match.
  • Aim specific first. A new site has a better chance with “wedding cakes in Lahore” than “cakes”.
  • One main keyword per page, then track the result.
